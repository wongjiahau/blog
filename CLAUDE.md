# Writing articles

## No process-related meta commentary in articles

Articles are written for readers, not for the author or the writing process. Do not put commentary about how the article was produced, researched, checked or revised into the published text. This applies everywhere in an article: body, quote citations, captions and the Sources section.

Do not write things like:

- "I paired the phrases by sense", "the Devanagari is my transcription", "I checked the originals against ..."
- "from memory", "I couldn't confirm ...", "I haven't verified ..."
- "the translations are literal, and in places rough"

Keep the notes a reader would expect: where a quotation comes from (work, chapter, edition), that translations are the author's own, and facts about the sources themselves (disputed attribution, textual variants, which translation is canonical).

If something is unverified, tell the author in chat instead of writing it into the article, and leave the claim out until it is verified.

# Evaluating an article

This is a checklist for re-running an evaluation on request, not a set of writing rules. Score each aspect (strong, adequate or weak) with one line of evidence, then list the weakest first.

1. **Purpose and thesis.** One claim, which the reader can state after the first page.
2. **Audience.** Clear who it is for and what they already know. Readers may not know Chinese or other source languages.
3. **Structure.** Each section does one job and leads into the next, in the order of the reader's questions.
4. **Logical soundness.** Each step follows from the previous one, claims are supported, and the strongest objections are answered.
5. **Economy.** Every sentence adds something and nothing is said twice. Removing a sentence should remove substance.
6. **Concreteness.** Abstractions are anchored by examples or scenes.
7. **Clarity.** Terms are used consistently and introduced before they are relied on. The prose is plain.
8. **Voice and honesty.** Claims are sized to the evidence, and interpretation is marked as interpretation.
9. **Evidence and sources.** Quotes are accurate and cited, with the original beside the translation.
10. **Rhythm and closing.** Sentence lengths vary, the opening earns attention, and the ending leaves the reader something to do or hold on to.
